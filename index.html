The Moment We're In

Jewish teens are being asked to defend who they are.

In classrooms, online, and among peers, they encounter distorted history, denial of Jewish peoplehood, and antisemitism disguised as political discourse. Even teens active in Jewish life may lack a clear understanding of Jewish history and Israel's centrality to Jewish identity.

This program supplements — not replaces — existing Jewish educational opportunities. It's built so every teen — whether they've grown up deeply immersed in Jewish life or are just starting to explore it — walks in and feels like it's for them.

"We cannot expect young people to stand confidently in their story if we have not first helped them know it." — Haviv Retig Gur, Senior Analyst, The Times of Israel

A Few Things
You Might Be Wondering

Who is Know Your Story for?

Every Jewish teen in the Twin Cities, grades 8–12 — regardless of denomination, affiliation, or how connected they currently feel to Jewish life. We've made sure to build it so that all teens feel welcomed, comfortable, and confident being themselves at Know Your Story.

What's the time commitment?

Three 90-minute sessions across the 2026–2027 school year — plus time for pizza and a giveaway drawing!

Does my teen need a Jewish education background?

No. Sessions are designed to meet teens where they are, whether they've had years of Jewish schooling or almost none.

Is there a cost?

There's a nominal cost to help cover food and materials, with financial assistance available. More details closer to launch.

How do I get my teen involved?

Reach out to knowyourstorymn@gmail.com or use the Get Involved button below — we'll follow up with registration details.

A Community Effort

This only works if we build it together.

Know Your Story is innovated by local Jewish educators, professionals, and volunteers, and brought to life in partnership with community organizations across every movement and denomination.

The goal is not to duplicate or recreate what already exists. It's to do together what none of us can do alone: reach every Jewish teen in the Twin Cities and give them what they need before they leave for college.
